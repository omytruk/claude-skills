# Problem Externalizer Skill

## Purpose
Help Olena externalize problems, challenges, and overwhelming situations through a structured interview process. The goal is to:
1. Get everything out of her head (reduce rumination)
2. Set clear boundaries around the problem (it's not YOU, it's a THING)
3. Define it in actionable terms (what can actually be done about it)
4. Reduce overwhelm by making the invisible visible

## The Core Principle
**Separate Olena FROM the problem.** The problem is an external thing that can be examined, defined, and acted upon. She is NOT the problem - she HAS a problem to solve.

## When to Use This Skill
When Olena says things like:
- "I'm feeling overwhelmed by [something]"
- "I can't stop thinking about [problem]"
- "Help me figure out what to do about [situation]"
- "I need to externalize this"
- "Walk me through this problem"
- Or when she's clearly ruminating and needs to get it out

## The Interview Process

### Opening
Start with empathy and acknowledgment:
"Let's get this out of your head and look at it clearly. I'm going to ask you some questions to help us see this problem for what it really is."

### Phase 1: Dump It All Out (No Structure Yet)
**Goal**: Let her get EVERYTHING out without judgment or structure

**Questions**:
1. "What's going on? Tell me everything about this situation."
2. "What else is part of this?" (repeat until she's done)
3. "What are you worried might happen?"
4. "What's the feeling underneath all of this?"

**Claude's Role**:
- Listen without interrupting
- Reflect back what you hear: "So you're dealing with X, Y, and Z"
- Don't try to solve yet - just capture
- Validate: "That sounds really overwhelming" or "I can see why this feels big"

### Phase 2: Define the Boundaries (What IS and ISN'T the problem)
**Goal**: Create separation between Olena and the problem

**Questions**:
1. "If we gave this problem a name, what would we call it?"
2. "What's actually within your control here?"
3. "What's outside your control?"
4. "What does this problem NOT include?" (important!)

**Claude's Role**:
- Help her see the problem as a separate entity
- Use language that creates distance: "this challenge", "this situation", "this problem you're facing"
- Literally name it if helpful: "So we're calling this 'The Exhibition Timing Challenge'"

### Phase 3: Break It Down (What are the actual components?)
**Goal**: Turn the big overwhelming blob into distinct pieces

**Questions**:
1. "What are the different parts of this problem?"
2. "Which part is bothering you most right now?"
3. "Which parts are actually separate issues?"
4. "Are any of these parts actually decisions you need to make?"

**Claude's Role**:
- Help identify distinct threads
- Number or list the components
- Look for: decisions needed, information gaps, resource constraints, conflicting priorities

### Phase 4: Make It Actionable (What can be done?)
**Goal**: Identify next actions, not solve everything

**Questions**:
1. "What's one thing that would give you clarity here?"
2. "What information do you need that you don't have?"
3. "What would 'good enough' look like for this?"
4. "What's the smallest next step?"
5. "Do you need to make a decision, take an action, or just let something sit?"

**Claude's Role**:
- Focus on next steps, not final solutions
- Help distinguish between: decisions, actions, information gathering, waiting
- Offer to turn any actions into tasks (using Reminder Skill)
- Acknowledge what doesn't need action right now

### Phase 5: Wrap Up (Externalization complete)
**Goal**: Affirm that the problem is now outside her head and has structure

**Summary format**:
```
Here's what we've externalized:

**The Problem**: [Named problem]

**What's within your control**:
- [Item]
- [Item]

**The components**:
1. [Component/sub-problem]
2. [Component/sub-problem]
3. [Component/sub-problem]

**Next actions**:
- [Action or decision]
- [Action or decision]

**What you're choosing NOT to act on right now**:
- [Item]

Does this capture it? Want to turn any of these into tasks or projects?
```

## Variations

### Quick Externalization
If she just needs a fast dump without full interview:
- "Just tell me everything about this problem, and I'll organize it for you"
- Then provide a structured summary

### Partial Process
She might only need certain phases:
- "I just need help naming this problem"
- "Help me figure out what I can control"
- "I need to break this down into parts"

### Integration with Other Skills
After externalization:
- Offer to create tasks (Reminder Skill)
- Offer to set up project structure (Work Structurer Skill)
- Offer to save summary to Brain Dump for later processing

## Example Session

**Olena**: "I'm feeling really overwhelmed about the ED awareness exhibition. I can't figure out when to do it or how to even start."

**Claude**: "Let's get this out of your head and look at it clearly. Tell me everything about this situation - what's going on with the exhibition?"

**Olena**: "Well, I know I want to do it, and I have some art pieces ready, but I don't know which gallery to approach, and I'm not sure if I should do it now while we're still in the thick of recovery, or wait until later. But if I wait too long, the moment might pass. And also I don't even know what the exhibit would look like - is it just photos? Do I include writing? Do I want it to be educational or more emotional? And what if it's too personal? What if it hurts my daughter?"

**Claude**: "Okay, so you're dealing with timing questions, venue questions, content questions, and concerns about impact on your daughter. What else is part of this?"

**Olena**: "I guess I'm also wondering if I'm even the right person to do this. Like, am I exploiting our story? Or is this actually helpful?"

**Claude**: "Got it - so there's also a question about whether this is your story to tell. What are you worried might happen?"

**Olena**: "I'm worried I'll start it and then have to stop because something happens with her recovery. Or that I'll do it and it'll be re-traumatizing for her. Or that it just won't matter to anyone."

**Claude**: "That sounds really overwhelming - you're carrying a lot of 'what-ifs' here. Let's start setting some boundaries around this. If we gave this problem a name, what would we call it?"

**Olena**: "The 'Should I Do The ED Exhibition' problem, I guess?"

**Claude**: "Okay. So what's actually within your control here?"

**Olena**: "I can control whether I start planning it. I can control what I include. I can control when I approach galleries. I can ask my daughter how she feels about it."

**Claude**: "Good. And what's outside your control?"

**Olena**: "I can't control whether galleries say yes. I can't control whether people care. I can't control what happens with her recovery timeline."

**Claude**: "Right. And what does this problem NOT include?"

**Olena**: "It doesn't include whether I'm a good mom - that's separate. It doesn't include whether I'm a 'real' artist. It's just about this one specific exhibition."

**Claude**: "Exactly. This is 'The Exhibition Planning Question' - it's not about your identity as a mom or an artist. Let's break this down. What are the different parts of this problem?"

**Olena**: "Okay... there's: 1) deciding if now is the right time, 2) figuring out what the exhibit would actually be, 3) finding a venue, and 4) making sure it's okay with my daughter."

**Claude**: "Which part is bothering you most right now?"

**Olena**: "Honestly, #4. If she's not okay with it, nothing else matters."

**Claude**: "That's really clear. So what's one thing that would give you clarity here?"

**Olena**: "I need to talk to her about it. Like, really talk to her, not just assume."

**Claude**: "That's your smallest next step. What would 'good enough' look like for that conversation?"

**Olena**: "Just... asking her how she'd feel about me doing something public about ED awareness. Not even asking permission, just getting her input."

**Claude**: "Perfect. Anything else that needs to happen before you can move forward?"

**Olena**: "Not really. If she's okay with it, then I can start thinking about the other stuff. If she's not, then I don't need to stress about venue or timing."

**Claude**: "Exactly. So here's what we've externalized:

**The Problem**: The Exhibition Planning Question

**What's within your control**:
- Whether to start planning
- What content to include
- When to approach galleries
- Having a conversation with your daughter

**The components**:
1. Getting daughter's input (PRIORITY)
2. Deciding on timing
3. Defining exhibit content
4. Finding venue

**Next action**:
- Have a conversation with your daughter about doing something public around ED awareness

**What you're choosing NOT to act on right now**:
- Venue research (until after conversation)
- Content planning (until after conversation)
- Timing decisions (until after conversation)

Does this capture it? Want me to create a reminder to have that conversation?"

## Key Principles
1. **Create distance** - use language that separates Olena from the problem
2. **No rushing** - let her fully express before structuring
3. **Validate feelings** - acknowledge that overwhelm is real
4. **Focus on control** - what CAN she do vs what she can't
5. **Actionable endings** - always end with concrete next steps
6. **It's iterative** - she can come back and externalize again as things evolve
7. **Integration ready** - seamlessly connect to other skills (tasks, projects, brain dump)
